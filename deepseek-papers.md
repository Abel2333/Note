## The thesis: the paper timeline is not the model timeline

**Techniques that appeared in papers months before landing in a model:**

- *Auxiliary-loss-free load balancing* — standalone paper **2024-08-28** ([2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664)). The DeepSeek-V3 report, **2024-12-27**, states in its abstract that V3 "pioneers an auxiliary-loss-free strategy for load balancing." Four months paper-only, then flagship adoption.
- *mHC* — paper **2025-12-31** ([2512.24880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880)) names no model. The V4 report ([2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348), **2026-04-26**) lists mHC as one of three headline upgrades. Theory first, landing later.
- *Engram* — paper **2026-01-12** ([2601.07372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372)). The V4.1-Flash card (**2026-09-10**) and report ([2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)) both carry a 196B-parameter Engram component.
- *GRPO* — introduced in DeepSeekMath **2024-02-05** as a 7B math-model training trick. Still the RL algorithm of record in V3.2 (**2025-12-02**), whose report contains a subsection headed "Scaling GRPO" — 22 months of reuse.
- *NSA* — published **2025-02-16** ([2502.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089)) as a 27B research-backbone study. Whether it landed in any released model is **UNKNOWN**; DSA is a separate line.

**Techniques and models that shipped before — or entirely without — a paper:**

- *DSA* debuted in the V3.2-Exp **model** on **2025-09-29**, documented only by a model card and a non-arXiv PDF. The arXiv write-up ([2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)) arrived **2025-12-02** and states the final V3.2 "uses exactly the same architecture as DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp."
- *DSpark* shipped as checkpoints **2026-06-27**, was already running "under live user traffic," and its paper ([2607.05147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05147)) came **2026-07-06** — nine days later.
- *DeepSeek-V3.1* (**2025-08-21**) and *V3.1-Terminus* (**2025-09-22**) have **no paper at all** — confirmed four ways: arXiv title query → 0 results; `au:"DeepSeek-AI"` over a window covering both launches → only the V3.2 paper; no "Technical Report" link on the card; 58 all-field hits, all third-party.
- *R1-0528* (**2025-05-28**) is a large capability jump — AIME 2025 from 70.0 to 87.5 — with no report of any kind. Its only paper reference is the original R1 report from four months earlier, which does not describe it.
- *V2.5*, *V2.5-1210*, *V3-0324*, and the V2 checkpoint series (0517/0614/0628) are changelog-and-card-only.
- *3FS*, *DeepEP*, *EPLB*, *DualPipe*, and *LPLB* are GitHub repositories. DualPipe's repo says it was "introduced in the DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report" and its only citation entry is `eprint={2412.19437}`.

**Corollary:** the citable surface is ~33 arXiv documents, but the *explanatory* surface includes model cards, release notes, and READMEs carrying mechanisms no paper states. Section VI marks that boundary.

Two secondary patterns. **Author lists are anomalously large and growing**: 200 entries for V3, 200 for R1, 264 for V3.2, 319 for V4, 593 for V4.1-Flash, 131 for a *sandbox infrastructure report* (DSec). Wenfeng Liang is a named author on V3, R1, V3.2, V4, V4.1-Flash, and DSec, and is the arXiv submitter on most — but is **absent** from DeepSeekMath (11 authors), DeepSeek-OCR (3), DeepSeek-OCR 2 (3), DeepSeek-VL (15), and DeepSeekMath-V2 (9). And **model cards routinely mis-tag their arXiv references**: the Coder-V2 cards tag DeepSeekMoE, the V2 card tags AlignBench (a THUDM benchmark), the DSpark cards tag the V4 report rather than the DSpark paper. Section VI warns about each.

## I. Foundation and scaling (2023–2024)

### DeepSeek LLM: Scaling Open-Source Language Models with Longtermism
[arXiv:2401.02954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02954) · 2024-01-05 (v1, only version) · ✅ verified-page · *foundation*

- **Why:** Kaplan et al. and Hoffmann et al./Chinchilla disagreed on compute allocation and gave incomplete hyperparameter descriptions. This re-derives scaling laws *including* hyperparameter scaling, then builds open 7B and 67B base and chat models.
- **Mechanisms:** `B_opt = 0.2920·C^0.3271` and `eta_opt = 0.3118·C^-0.1250`; new model-scale measure `M = 72·n_layer·d_model² + 12·n_layer·d_model·l_seq` replacing `C = 6ND` with `C = MD`; IsoFLOP-profile fitting (from Chinchilla); multi-step LR scheduler instead of cosine (peak after 2000 warmup steps, 31.6% of peak at 80% of tokens, 10% at 90%) chosen so phase one can be reused for continual training; LLaMA micro-design (Pre-Norm, RMSNorm, SwiGLU at 8/3·d_model, RoPE), with 67B using GQA and spending parameters on depth (95 layers); SFT on >1M instances then DPO.
- **Numbers:** 2.0T tokens for both sizes. 7B: 30 layers, d_model 4096, 32/32 heads, batch 2304, LR 4.2e-4. 67B: 95 layers, d_model 8192, 64 heads / 8 kv_heads, batch 4608, LR 3.2e-4. Common Crawl dedup rises 22.2% (1 dump) → 89.8% (91 dumps). Tokenizer 100,000 conventional + 15 special on ~24 GB, model vocab 102400. AdamW (β1 0.9, β2 0.95, wd 0.1), init std 0.006, clip 1.0. Claimed: 67B surpasses LLaMA-2 70B; 67B Chat beats GPT-3.5 on open-ended evaluation.
- **Timing:** Same-release for its own models. DeepSeekMath later states it initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B instead.
- **Caveats:** Fetch truncated inside §3.2, so scaling exponents *a*/*b* and all score tables were **not read**. 86 authors counted on the abs page; Wenfeng Liang is 28th.

### DeepSeek-Coder: When the Large Language Model Meets Programming — The Rise of Code Intelligence
[arXiv:2401.14196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14196) · 2024-01-25 (v1); v2 2024-01-26 · ✅ verified-page · *foundation*

- **Why:** Code intelligence was closed-source-dominated. This releases an open family under a permissive commercial license, claiming parity with Codex and GPT-3.5 on code benchmarks.
- **Mechanisms:** Repository-level data construction — dependencies extracted by regex (`import` in Python, `using` in C#, `include` in C), files ordered by a modified topological sort picking minimal-in-degree nodes so cycles resolve, path comment prepended, each sorted sequence one sample; repo-level near-deduplication of the concatenated repository rather than per file; Fill-In-Middle objective alongside next-token prediction; 16K context; public GitHub repos created before February 2023, filtered with StarCoder-like rules; Instruct variants at each size.
- **Numbers:** 1.3B–33B from scratch on 2T tokens over 87 languages. Filtering reduces crawled data to **32.8%**. Samples read: Python 120.68 GB / 75,188k files / 15.12%; C++ 90.87 GB; PHP 58.92 GB / 40,627k files / 7.38%; C# 58.56 GB / 53,739k files / 7.34%; C 28.64 GB / 27,111k files / 3.59%. Thresholds: avg line >100 chars or max >1000 removed; <25% alphabetic removed; HTML kept only if visible text ≥20% and ≥100 chars; JSON/YAML only between 50 and 5000 chars. Mix 87% code / 10% English code-related NL / 3% code-unrelated Chinese NL. Claimed: Base 33B superior to all open-source; Instruct 33B beats GPT-3.5 Turbo on a majority of benchmarks; Base 7B competitive with CodeLlama-33B at ~1/5 the size.
- **Timing:** Same-release for its own 1.3B–33B series. Cross-paper: DeepSeekMath-Base initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, so the code model preceded the math model. Other later adoption: **UNKNOWN**.
- **Caveats:** Truncated mid-§2.3; no HumanEval/MBPP/DS-1000 numbers asserted. Abs-page title uses a double hyphen; HTML uses a single. 13 authors, Wenfeng Liang last and submitter.

### DeepSeek-V2: A Strong, Economical, and Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Language Model
[arXiv:2405.04434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434) · v1 2024-05-07; v5 2024-06-19 · ✅ verified-page · *foundation*

- **Why:** Attacked both cost centers at once — expensive training and KV-cache-bound inference — by redesigning attention *and* the FFN of a 236B MoE. Canonical source for MLA and the V2 form of DeepSeekMoE.
- **Mechanisms:** **MLA** low-rank KV joint compression: `c_t^KV = W^DKV h_t` with `d_c << d_h·n_h`, so inference caches `d_c·l` elements instead of `2·n_h·d_h·l`; `W^UK` absorbs into `W^Q`, `W^UV` into `W^O`, so K/V need never be materialized. **Decoupled RoPE** — RoPE is position-sensitive and breaks low-rank absorption, so it rides additional multi-head queries. Low-rank query compression (`c_t^Q = W^DQ h_t`) explicitly to cut *training* activation memory (does not reduce KV cache). DeepSeekMoE with fine-grained segmentation + shared expert isolation, Device-Limited Routing, three balance losses (expert-level, device-level, communication), Token-Dropping Strategy. Yarn extension to 128K.
- **Numbers:** 236B total / 21B activated; 128K context; 8.1T tokens. vs DeepSeek 67B: **42.5% training-cost saving, 93.3% KV-cache reduction, 5.76× max generation throughput**. 1.5M conversational sessions for SFT, then GRPO. Chat (RL): 38.9 LC win rate AlpacaEval 2.0, 8.97 MT-Bench, 7.91 AlignBench. V2-Lite: 15.7B / 2.4B.
- **Timing:** Primary description of MLA and the V2 MoE — with the V2 release. Verified downstream: DeepSeek-Coder-V2 (2024-06-17) is "further pre-trained from an intermediate checkpoint of DeepSeek-V2," reuses V2's GRPO and Yarn hyper-parameters, and matches its 16B to V2-Lite. MLA in V3/R1 was **UNKNOWN** from these pages (the V3 report, fetched separately, states MLA/DeepSeekMoE/MTP were "thoroughly validated in DeepSeek-V2").
- **Caveats:** Five versions; "2024-05-07" is v1 only. Abs page truncates with "(57 additional authors not shown)". Page spells one name "Chengqi Dengr." Fetch truncated inside §2.1.3.

### DeepSeek-Coder-V2: Breaking the Barrier of Closed-Source Models in Code Intelligence
[arXiv:2406.11931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11931) · 2024-06-17 (v1 only) · ✅ verified-page · *other*

- **Why:** Open code models trailed GPT4-Turbo/Claude 3 Opus/Gemini 1.5 Pro, and DeepSeek-Coder topped out at 33B/86 languages/16K. This delivered the first open hundred-billion-parameter code model the authors report as beating those closed models; its recipe became the template for the code line.
- **Mechanisms:** Continued pre-training from a V2 intermediate checkpoint (already 4.2T tokens) on 6T more = 10.2T; mix 60% code / 10% math / 30% NL. Architecture identical to V2 (16B ≈ V2-Lite, 236B ≈ V2). Reports training instability and gradient spikes attributed to exponential normalization, reverted to conventional normalization. Code/math web recall borrowed from DeepSeekMath: seed with StackOverflow/PyTorch docs/math StackExchange, fastText classifier on the V2 BPE tokenizer, three domain-expansion iterations plus two GitHub rounds. Yarn extension (s=40, α=1, β=32) in two stages, validated by NIAH to 128K. FIM in PSM mode at rate 0.5, **16B model only**. Alignment: SFT 300M tokens, then GRPO with a reward model rather than raw compiler 0/1 feedback (test cases give incomplete coverage; compiler signal is noisy).
- **Numbers:** HumanEval 90.2%, MBPP+ 76.2% (EvalPlus, stated new SOTA), LiveCodeBench 43.4% (Dec 2023–Jun 2024). First open model reported to surpass 10% on SWE-bench. MATH 75.7% vs GPT-4o 76.6%; MMLU 79.2%; arena-hard 65.0; MT-bench 8.77; alignbench 7.84. Sizes 16B/2.4B and 236B/21B. Languages 86→338; context 16K→128K. Tokens 4.2T + 6T = 10.2T; new corpus 1,170B code-related, filtered to 821B code + 185B text, plus 70B code-related and 221B math-related from web. 1B ablation: HumanEval 30.5→37.2, MBPP 44.6→54.0. Multilingual HumanEval (236B instruct): Python 90.2, Java 82.3, C++ 84.8, C# 82.3, TypeScript 83.0, JavaScript 84.5, PHP 79.5, Bash 52.5; Lite 16B instruct Python 81.1.
- **Timing:** Explicit paper-after-paper dependency: after V2, built as continued pre-training from a V2 intermediate checkpoint while reusing V2's architecture, tokenizer, GRPO alignment, and Yarn hyper-parameters. So V2's techniques demonstrably landed in a later model/paper. Coder-V2's own pipeline reuse: **UNKNOWN**.
- **Caveats:** Alternates between "DeepSeek-Coder-V2", "DeepSeek-Coder-v2", "DeepSeek V2" in the same sections. Fetch truncated inside §4.1; §4.2–4.6 tables not read. 29+ named authors plus the DeepSeek-AI collective, **Wenfeng Liang last**, no "et al." truncation.

### DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report
[arXiv:2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437) · v1 2024-12-27; v2 2025-02-18 · ✅ verified-page · *foundation*

- **Why:** The 671B MoE whose architecture and training stack became the reference open-weight frontier model, at a stated training cost of the same order as far smaller budgets.
- **Mechanisms:** MLA with low-rank joint KV compression; only the compressed latent and decoupled RoPE key are cached. DeepSeekMoE with **auxiliary-loss-free load balancing** (bias-based), a Complementary Sequence-Wise Auxiliary Loss, Node-Limited Routing, No Token-Dropping. **Multi-Token Prediction** with dedicated modules, also usable for speculative decoding. **DualPipe** pipeline parallelism with computation-communication overlap, plus cross-node all-to-all kernels over InfiniBand and NVLink. **FP8 mixed precision**: fine-grained quantization, increased accumulation precision, mantissa over exponents, online quantization, low-precision optimizer states/activations/communication.
- **Numbers:** 671B total / 37B activated; 14.8T tokens. **2.788M H800 GPU hours** = 2664K pre-training + 119K context extension + 5K post-training; **$5.576M** at $2/H800-hour. 180K H800 hours per trillion tokens = 3.7 days on 2048 H800s. Context 32K → 128K. Chat: MMLU 88.5, MMLU-Pro 75.9, GPQA 59.1.
- **Timing:** MTP/MLA/DeepSeekMoE are stated as "thoroughly validated in DeepSeek-V2," i.e. pre-date this. The v2 text (2025-02-18) says post-training "distill[s] the reasoning capability from the DeepSeek-R1 series," so that content post-dates R1's v1 (2025-01-22).
- **Caveats:** Abs page truncates with "(100 additional authors not shown)"; arXiv API counts **200 author entries**; Wenfeng Liang is the submitter. Fetch truncated inside §2.1.1 — DualPipe, FP8, MTP, node-limited routing and no-token-dropping were read as section headings and Introduction narrative only. DualPipe is verified present in the report's TOC as "3.2.1 DualPipe and Computation-Communication Overlap."

## II. Architecture: MoE, MLA, and attention (V2 → V3 → NSA → V3.2 DSA → Engram → V4)

### DeepSeekMoE: Towards Ultimate Expert Specialization in Mixture-of-Experts Language Models
[arXiv:2401.06066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066) · 2024-01-11 (v1 only) · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** Conventional MoE (GShard-style top-K of N) suffers knowledge hybridity and redundancy, preventing specialization. This restructures expert granularity and adds always-on shared experts at equal parameter and compute cost.
- **Mechanisms:** **Fine-grained expert segmentation** — each FFN expert split into *m* smaller experts by reducing intermediate hidden dim to 1/m, with activated experts raised to *mK* of *mN* so parameters and compute stay constant. **Shared expert isolation** — `K_s` experts deterministically always-activated for common knowledge, with routed experts reduced by `K_s`. Two named auxiliary losses: expert-level balance loss and device-level balance loss. Combinatorial argument: N=16, top-2 gives C(16,2)=**120** possible activated-expert combinations; splitting each into 4 and routing top-8 gives C(64,8)=**4,426,165,368**.
- **Numbers:** 2B matches GShard 2.9B (which has 1.5× the expert parameters and computation); 2B nearly approaches its dense same-total-parameter counterpart. 16B on a 2T corpus matches DeepSeek 7B (dense, same corpus) and LLaMA2 7B using only **~40%** of computations; LLaMA2 7B has ~2.5× the activated parameters. 145B comparable with DeepSeek 67B at **28.5% (maybe 18.2%)** of computations. 12 zero/few-shot benchmarks. The 16B checkpoint deploys on a single 40GB GPU without quantization.
- **Timing:** Technique and its own models same-release. Nothing on the fetched pages states adoption by a later model — adoption is cross-paper verified (V2, V3, VL2, OCR all name DeepSeekMoE), not same-paper verified.
- **Caveats:** Truncated inside §3.2; closing formulas and hyperparameter appendix not read; no score tables. Printed footnote ordering on the HTML first page is garbled, so only the unambiguous fact is asserted: Wenfeng Liang listed last with a contact email. 17 authors.

### Expert-Specialized Fine-Tuning (ESFT): Let the Expert Stick to His Last
[arXiv:2407.01906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01906) · v1 2024-07-02; v2 2024-07-05 · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** PEFT research had focused on dense LLMs, leaving sparse MoE underexplored. This shows MoE routing is task-concentrated and exploits that to tune only task-relevant experts, matching or beating full fine-tuning at lower cost.
- **Mechanisms:** Only the experts most relevant to the downstream task in each MoE layer are updated; everything else frozen, while tokens can still route anywhere. Two relevance scores: **ESFT-Gate** (average gate score over sampled tokens) and **ESFT-Token** (token selection ratio), with threshold *p* ∈ (0,1] selecting per-layer the top-scored experts whose cumulative relevance reaches *p*. Selection uses only 32 concatenated samples of fixed length L=4096. Probing: normalized gate values concentrate in a small expert subset; shared Top-6 routed sets are consistent within a task (diagonal near 6) but nearly disjoint across tasks (off-diagonal near 0). Finer-grained segmentation plus shared-expert isolation makes task-relevant selection *easier*.
- **Numbers:** Storage reduced up to **90%**, training time up to **30%**. Selecting **5–15%** of experts performs well across tasks. Average over 4 task groups: ESFT-Gate 50.2, ESFT-Token 49.4, FFT 51.0, LoRA 44.9, vanilla 33.6. ESFT-Gate vs FFT per task (ESFT wins two): MATH 23.2/23.4, GSM8K 64.9/66.4, HumanEval 43.3/42.1, MBPP 41.8/42.2, Intent 78.6/78.8, Summarization 65.8/69.4, Law judgment 49.1/47.0, Translation 35.2/38.4.
- **Timing:** Evaluated on the MoE family it cites as prior work (V2 162 experts / 8 active; V2-Lite 8 of 66), so the sparse architecture pre-dates the method. Adoption later: **UNKNOWN**.
- **Caveats:** 6 authors; Wenfeng Liang **not** among them; code at `github.com/deepseek-ai/ESFT`. Fetch truncated during the general-ability results.

### Auxiliary-Loss-Free Load Balancing Strategy for Mixture-of-Experts
[arXiv:2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664) · 2024-08-28 (v1 only) · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** MoE load imbalance causes routing collapse and compute bottlenecks, but the standard auxiliary loss injects interference gradients that hurt quality. This removes the trade-off with a dynamic per-expert bias instead of a loss term — and became V3's scheme.
- **Mechanisms:** **Loss-Free Balancing** adds expert-wise bias `{b_i}` to gating scores and uses `s_i,t + b_i` for the top-K selection. The bias steers only the top-K *decision*; it is explicitly **not** added to `g_i,t` weighting outputs. Iterative update from historical load only: `e_i = mean(c_i) − c_i`, then `b_i = b_i + u·sign(e_i)`; using the current sequence's load would break causality and leak future-token information. Backbone is DeepSeekMoE with a **sigmoid gate** (beat softmax in the main experiments; softmax in Appendix C). Claimed natural compatibility with expert parallelism. Metric `MaxVio = (max_i Load_i − mean(Load_i))/mean(Load_i)`, global and batch, averaged over layers.
- **Numbers:** 1B MoE / 100B tokens: perplexity **9.50** vs 9.56 (Loss-Controlled); MaxVio_global **0.04** vs 0.72. 3B / 200B: 7.92 vs 7.97; MaxVio_global 0.04 vs 0.52. Baseline α = 0.001 (swept 1e-2/1e-3/1e-4/0). Update rate u = 0.001 optimal (1e-4 too slow, 1e-2 fluctuates late). Alternative `b_i + u·e_i` at u=0.01: perplexity 9.53, MaxVio 0.028 — better balance, no gain, so the sign version was kept. BPE vocab 32K.
- **Timing:** **Verified paper-before-model.** Dated 2024-08-28; the V3 report (2024-12-27) states V3 "pioneers an auxiliary-loss-free strategy for load balancing" — module-level adoption ~4 months later.
- **Caveats:** 5 authors. Truncated during §4.3; Tables 3+ and Appendices A–D not read. The Expert Choice leakage claim is the paper's own Table 1/§5.2, not independent reproduction.

### Native Sparse Attention: Hardware-Aligned and Natively Trainable Sparse Attention
[arXiv:2502.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089) · v1 2025-02-16; v2 2025-02-27 · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** Sparse attention rarely converts theoretical sparsity into real latency and mostly cannot be trained end-to-end. NSA makes sparsity native from pre-training with hardware-aligned kernels, treating memory-bound decode and compute-bound prefill/training separately.
- **Mechanisms:** Three parallel branches with a learned gate `o*_t = Σ_c g^c_t · Attn(q_t, K̃^c_t, Ṽ^c_t)` over compression/selection/sliding-window, gates from an MLP + sigmoid, each branch with independent K/V to prevent shortcut learning and gradient interference. Token compression: learnable MLP φ with intra-block position encoding aggregates each block of length *l* (stride d < l) into one compressed K/V. Blockwise selection with **reused importance scores** taken from the compression branch's softmax (no extra scoring pass), remapped to selection blocks, top-n retained — block-level, not token-level, for Tensor Core utilization. **GQA/MQA group-consistent selection**: importance summed across the H query heads of a group so all heads select the same blocks, making memory-access sparsity match compute sparsity (explicitly contrasted with Quest). Triton kernel: Group-Centric Data Loading, Shared KV Fetching, Outer Loop on Grid. Natively trainable by design — rejects non-differentiable components (ClusterKV's k-means, MagicPIG's SimHash) and token-granular selection (HashAttention).
- **Numbers:** Backbone 27B total / **3B active**, 30 layers, hidden 2560; GQA 4 groups, 64 heads, d_q=d_k=192, d_v=128; DeepSeekMoE 72 routed + 2 shared, top-k 6, first-layer MoE replaced by SwiGLU MLP. **Inconsistency in the paper: the Introduction says 260B tokens, §4.1 says 270B** for the same models. Hyperparameters l=32, d=16, l'=64, n=16 (1 initial + 2 local fixed), w=512. Full-attention → NSA: average 0.443→0.456; MMLU 0.567→0.565; MMLU-PRO 0.279→0.286; CMMLU 0.576→0.587; BBH 0.497→0.521; GSM8K 0.486→0.520; MATH 0.263→0.264; DROP 0.503→0.545; MBPP 0.482→0.466; HumanEval 0.335→0.348. Motivating estimates: attention = 70–80% of decode latency at 64K; top 20% of attention covers only 70% of attention scores.
- **Timing:** Post-dates the V3 report, which it cites; evaluates a research backbone only and never claims to ship. V3-0324 (2025-03-24, *after* this) states its structure "is exactly the same as DeepSeek-V3," so not an NSA adoption. Later adoption: **UNKNOWN**.
- **Caveats:** 15 authors; Wenfeng Liang is **14th of 15**, not last. §5 efficiency analysis (the speedup multipliers) lies beyond the fetch truncation on both the arXiv HTML and the ar5iv mirror, so those multipliers are **UNVERIFIED**; the ACL PDF returned "unsupported content type." A third-party reading-notes page reports 64K speedups of 11.6× decode / 9.0× forward / 6.0× backward — **not confirmed against the paper**, therefore not asserted.

### DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) — introduced in V3.2-Exp, documented in DeepSeek-V3.2
Release [news 2025-09-29](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news250929/) · paper [arXiv:2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556), 2025-12-02 · ✅ verified-page · *architecture / reasoning-RL*

- **Why:** Defines every DeepSeek model from V3.2-Exp onward, and is the clearest instance of model-before-paper in the portfolio.
- **Mechanisms:** DSA = a **lightning indexer** scoring query-token/prior-token pairs (ReLU "for throughput consideration," few heads, implementable in FP8) plus **fine-grained token selection** keeping only the top-k KV entries; main-model core attention drops from `O(L²)` to `O(L·k)`. Instantiated under MLA in its **MQA mode**, so each latent KV entry is shared across all query heads. Two-stage continued pre-training from a **V3.1-Terminus** base (128K): a *dense warm-up* freezing everything but the indexer and aligning it by KL divergence against the L1-normalized, head-summed main attention distribution; then *sparse training* activating selection and optimizing all parameters, with the indexer optimized only by its own KL loss and its input detached from the graph. ### mHC: Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections
[arXiv:2512.24880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880) · v1 2025-12-31; v2 2026-01-05 · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** Hyper-Connections widen and diversify the residual stream but the unconstrained residual mapping destroys the identity-mapping property, causing exploding/vanishing signals at scale plus heavy memory-access overhead. mHC restores it by constraining the residual mixing matrix to a manifold.
- **Mechanisms:** Project `H_l^res` onto the **Birkhoff polytope** of doubly stochastic matrices — `P_M(H) = {H | H·1 = 1, 1ᵀ·H = 1ᵀ, H ⩾ 0}` — using **Sinkhorn-Knopp** as the entropic projection, so `H_l^res x_l` is a convex combination with conserved feature mean. Stated properties: spectral norm ≤ 1 (non-expansive); closure under multiplication so the composite mapping stays doubly stochastic across depth (degenerating to the scalar 1 when n = 1); a Birkhoff-polytope reading as convex hull of permutation matrices = monotone feature mixing. Non-negativity also imposed on `H^pre` and `H^post` to prevent signal cancellation. Infrastructure: kernel fusion and mixed-precision kernels in TileLang, selective recomputing, overlapped communication inside DualPipe. New diagnostic: **"Amax Gain Magnitude"** of the composite mapping (max abs row sum = forward, max abs column sum = backward).
- **Numbers:** At expansion rate n = 4, **6.7% additional time overhead**. HC's composite Amax Gain Magnitude reaches "extreme values with **peaks of 3000**" in the 27B model versus an ideal of 1. HC shows an unexpected loss surge "around the **12k step**," correlated with gradient-norm instability. Component ablation (absolute loss gap): 0.0 none, −0.022 `H^res`, −0.025 `H^res`+`H^pre`, −0.027 all three. Per-token residual-stream memory access: standard 2C read / C write; HC `(5n+1)C + n² + 2n` read and `(3n+1)C + n² + 2n` write — ~n-fold more I/O. Typical n ≈ 4 ≪ C.
- **Timing:** Names no released model, so same-paper timing is **UNKNOWN**. Cross-paper landing: the V4 report (2026-04-26) lists mHC among its three headline upgrades; the V4.1-Flash report and card name "Single-Pass mHC" with a Mega-mHC kernel. Paper-first by ~4 months.
- **Caveats:** 20 authors, all DeepSeek-AI; **Wenfeng Liang is last author and submitter**. Both HTML mirrors truncated inside §4.2, so §5 (Experimental Setup, Main Results, Scaling, Stability) and Appendix A.1 were **not read**; no §5 benchmark scores or loss values asserted. ### Conditional Memory via Scalable Lookup: A New Axis of Sparsity (Engram)
[arXiv:2601.07372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372) · v1 2026-01-12; v2 2026-07-12 · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** Argues much of language is local, static, and stereotyped, so a dedicated lookup primitive should complement MoE's conditional computation rather than being simulated by depth.
- **Mechanisms:** Engram fuses retrieved static N-gram memory with dynamic hidden states via context-aware gating, applied to selected layers only, decoupling memory from compute. Sparse retrieval via hashed N-grams: tokenizer compression through `P: V → V'` (NFKC, lowercasing) plus K multi-head hashing per N-gram order into prime-sized tables using a multiplicative-XOR hash. Gating: hidden state as Query, retrieved memory as Key/Value, RMSNorm and a sigmoid scalar gate, then a depthwise causal Conv1D (kernel 4, dilation = max N-gram order) and SiLU. Integrated with **mHC (M=4)** multi-branch backbone using a shared embedding table and `W_V` with M branch-specific `W_K`, fusable into one dense FP8 matmul. Systems: All-to-All sharding for training, deterministic-address host-memory prefetch-and-overlap for inference, multi-level cache hierarchy motivated by Zipfian N-gram access.

Engram scaled to **27B parameters**. Multi-Query NIAH 84.2 → 97.0; Variable Tracking 89.0 vs 77.0. A **100B-parameter table offloaded to host memory with <3% overhead**. **23% reduction** in effective vocabulary size for a 128k tokenizer. U-shaped allocation curves at 2e20 and 6e20 FLOPs.

### DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression
[arXiv:2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969) · 2026-09-17 · ✅ verified-page · *architecture*

- **Why:** Long-horizon agent workloads are input-heavy, so prefill compute and KV-cache storage/bandwidth became the binding constraint on deployment cost. This attacks the bottleneck jointly through architecture, cache precision, and deployment strategy.
- **Mechanisms:** **Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED)**: decoder global KV is projected from the final encoder hidden state `H_{L/2}` via layer-dependent weights, so most prompt tokens bypass full decoder computation — prefill complexity drops from `O(NL)` to `O(NL/2 + n_win·L/2)` for N ≫ n_win. **CSA2**: cross-layer reuse of global KV (main KV + indexer K) and of Top-K indices, with three statically assigned modes — **Full** (generates global KV and indexes), **Reindex** (reuses the preceding layer's global KV but rescores shared indexer K with its own indexer Q), **Reuse** (reuses both and directly performs sparse attention); V4.1-Flash uses **pure CSA2** instead of V4's CSA+HCA hybrid. **FP4 main KV cache** used during training with only marginal degradation. **Single-Pass mHC** with a Mega-mHC kernel halving activation memory traffic. **SWA Bounded Replay**: approximately reconstructs SWA KV states by replaying only the most recent `n_win` tokens instead of needing `L·n_win`, avoiding persisting SWA KV to SSD at the cost of small prefill recomputation. Multimodal: from-scratch DeepSeek-ViT with 2D-RoPE, linear patch projection (for Muon compatibility), RMSNorm, SwiGLU, plus 3×3 pixel-unshuffle reducing visual tokens 9×; multimodal auxiliary-loss-free balancing keeping separate expert-wise correction biases for text and image tokens. Hierarchical Sparse Indexer restricts later decoder indexers to a candidate pool from an earlier indexer; each CSA2 Reuse-Mode layer runs only **15 kernels prefill / 11 decode**.
- **Numbers:** **552B backbone + 196B Engram**; activates **8B/token prefill, 16B/token decode**; up to **1M** context. Global KV **890 bytes/token**, roughly **1/4** of V4-Flash (~4-fold); ~**437-fold** vs DeepSeek-V1. Persistent KV ~**1/8** of V4-Flash via SWA Bounded Replay. Extending context 256-fold from 4K to 1M raises single-token decode FLOPs by only **1/4** (BF16/FP8/FP4 weighted 1/0.5/0.25). Pre-trained on **45T** multimodal tokens; sparse attention trained from scratch at 64K with **no dense-attention warmup**. 40 layers = 20 encoder + 20 decoder; first two encoder layers SWA, rest CSA2. V4.1-Flash-Base reported comparable world knowledge/reasoning/coding to V4-Pro-Base with 5–10% improvements on held-out evaluations, using 1/3 total and 1/4 activated parameters. The introduction claims capability on "over 95% of real-world tasks" — a text claim, not a measured score read.
- **Timing:** Compares against V4, V4-Flash, and V4-Pro as existing predecessors and cites DeepSeek-V4 as prior work. **Model-first**: API note 2026-09-10, HF repo created 2026-09-10T02:17:58Z, this report 2026-09-17 — 7 days later.

### DeepSeek-Prover: Advancing Theorem Proving in LLMs through Large-Scale Synthetic Data
[arXiv:2405.14333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14333) · 2024-05-23 (v1 only, never revised) · ✅ verified-page · *reasoning-RL*

- **Why:** Formal Lean proving was bottlenecked by scarce formal proof corpora. This shows large-scale synthetic autoformalization plus verification-driven filtering can push a **7B** model past GPT-4 on whole-proof generation.

### Inference-Time Scaling for Generalist Reward Modeling (DeepSeek-GRM)
[arXiv:2504.02495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02495) · v1 2025-04-03; v3 2025-09-25 · ✅ verified-page · *reasoning-RL*

### DeepSeek-Prover-V2: Advancing Formal Mathematical Reasoning via RL for Subgoal Decomposition

### DeepSeekMath-V2: Towards Self-Verifiable Mathematical Reasoning
[arXiv:2511.22570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22570) · 2025-11-27 (v1 only) · ✅ verified-page · *reasoning-RL*

- **Why:** RL rewarding only correct final answers cannot apply to theorem proving and does not guarantee correct reasoning, so models produce invalid proofs and cannot judge their own work. This trains an LLM verifier to find genuine flaws in NL proofs, uses it as the reward model for a proof generator, and scales verification compute to auto-label hard proofs.
- **Mechanisms:** **Verifier trained by RL against proof-evaluation rubrics**: given problem X and proof Y it emits a proof analysis and a 3-level score (1 = complete and rigorous, 0.5 = sound logic with minor errors/omitted details, 0 = fatal errors or critical gaps). **Two-part reward**: `R_format` enforcing the key phrase "Here is my evaluation of the solution:" plus a boxed score after "Based on my evaluation, the final overall score should be:", multiplied by `R_score(s', s) = 1 − |s' − s|` against expert annotations. **Meta-verification** to suppress hallucinated issues: a dedicated RL-trained meta-verifier judges whether identified issues actually exist and justify the predicted score under rubrics `I_mv`; the verifier's reward becomes `R_V = R_format · R_score · R_meta`. **Self-verification reward for the generator**: it produces proof Y plus self-analysis Z in the same format, with `R = R_format(Y,Z) · (α·R_Y + β·R_Z)` where `R_Z = R_score(s',s)·R_meta(Z)`, **α = 0.76 and β = 0.24** — incentivizing the model to find and fix its own issues before finalizing rather than claiming false correctness. **Scaling verification compute for automated labeling**: n independent verification analyses per proof, then m meta-verification assessments for analyses reporting issues (valid if a majority confirm); a proof takes the lowest score at least k valid analyses agree on, is labeled 1 if no legitimate issues are found, and is otherwise discarded or routed to human experts — this pipeline **replaced human annotation in the last two training iterations**. **Iterative GRPO loop with role synergy**: the verifier's generative reward optimizes the generator, the strengthened generator produces harder proofs, and those retrain the verifier; the generator is initialized from the verifier checkpoint and, from the second iteration, the verifier is initialized from a checkpoint consolidating both capabilities via rejection fine-tuning. **High-compute test-time search**: maintain a pool of candidate proofs (64 proof samples and 64 verification analyses each), refine by selecting the top-64 by average verification score, pair each with 8 randomly selected analyses (prioritizing issue-reporting ones), generate one refined proof per pair, and iterate up to 16 times or until a proof passes all 64 verification attempts.
- **Numbers:** **Putnam 2024: 118/120** with scaled test-time compute — 11 of 12 solved completely and the remaining one with minor errors — stated to exceed the highest human score of **90** (98.3% of points). **IMO 2025: gold-level, 5 of 6 fully solved** (P1–P5), 83.3% of points. **CMO 2024: gold-level**, 4 solved plus partial credit (P1, P2, P4, P5, P6), 73.8% of points. Verifier faithfulness: on a validation split of `D_v`, the meta-verifier's average quality score of the verifier's proof analyses improved **0.85 → 0.96** while keeping the same proof-score prediction accuracy. Cold start: **17,503** proof-requiring problems crawled from Art of Problem Solving contest collections, prioritizing olympiads, team selection tests, and post-2010 problems; candidate proofs generated with a variant of **DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp-Thinking** then expert-scored. Benchmarks: in-house CNML-level set of **91** problems (algebra 13, geometry 24, number theory 19, combinatorics 24, inequality 11); IMO 2025 and CMO 2024 (6 each); Putnam 2024 (12); ISL 2024 (31); IMO-ProofBench (60: 30 basic, 30 advanced). One-shot protocol: 8 proof samples per problem, correctness by majority vote over 8 verification analyses; the ISL 2024 sequential-refinement study used 32 independent refinement threads per problem, reported as Pass@1 and Best@32; proofs must fit a **128K token limit**. On IMO-ProofBench the model outperforms DeepMind's DeepThink (IMO Gold) on the **basic** set and remains competitive on the **advanced** set, with baseline values sourced from Luong et al. (2025) and DeepSeekMath-V2 graded by the authors' experts.
- **Timing:** **Paper after a prior model** — built on **DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp-Base**. Cites DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (2504.21801) for valid formal Lean proofs and Seed-Prover (2507.23726). The model is introduced with this paper; the HF repo was created 2025-11-27T09:54:23Z, ~6 h before the arXiv v1.

### Insights into DeepSeek-V3: Scaling Challenges and Reflections on Hardware for AI Architectures

### DSpark: Confidence-Scheduled Speculative Decoding with Semi-Autoregressive Generation
[arXiv:2607.05147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05147) · v1 2026-07-06 · ✅ verified-page · *systems-infra*

- **Why:** Targets the two real bottlenecks of long-block speculative decoding in production serving: acceptance decay in parallel drafters, and wasted target-model batch capacity spent verifying low-probability suffix tokens under high concurrency.
- **Mechanisms:** **Semi-autoregressive generation**: a fully parallel backbone handles the bulk of draft computation and a lightweight sequential block injects intra-block dependency. The parallel stage is instantiated on **DFlash**, modified so the anchor token itself is the first prediction position (anchor + γ−1 masks yield γ draft logits); the sequential stage factorizes the block causally as `p_k(v|x_0, x_<k) ∝ exp(U_k(v) + B_k(x_0, x_<k, v))`. Two sequential heads: a **Markov head** (transition bias depends only on the previous token, low-rank `B = W1·W2`, **r = 256 default**) and an **RNN head** (gated recurrent state over the whole in-block prefix). **Confidence-scheduled verification**: a confidence head estimates per-position prefix survival probabilities with post-hoc calibration, and a **Hardware-Aware Prefix Scheduler** converts those plus engine-specific throughput profiles into a per-request verification length, pruning low-confidence suffix tokens. Baseline mechanics: DFlash performs **KV injection**, forming `H_ctx = RMSNorm(W_c[H^(l1); …; H^(lm)])` from a set of target layers and concatenating them into the keys and values of every draft layer; the draft shares the frozen target embedding and LM head. Acceptance uses `min(1, p^t_k(x_k)/p^d_k(x_k))` with left-to-right prefix acceptance; latency modelled as `L = (T_draft + T_verify)/τ`. Open-sourced: DSpark checkpoints for V4-Flash and V4-Pro plus **DeepSpec**, including Eagle3, DFlash, and DSpark.
- **Numbers:** Per-user generation speed at matched aggregate throughput vs the **MTP-1** production baseline: **+60% to +85%** for V4-Flash and **+57% to +78%** for V4-Pro. Macro-average accepted length vs the autoregressive **Eagle3** drafter: **+30.9%** (Qwen3-4B), **+26.7%** (Qwen3-8B), **+30.0%** (Qwen3-14B); vs the parallel **DFlash** drafter: **+16.3%, +18.4%, +18.3%**. Strict-SLA operating points cited where baseline capacity deteriorates severely: **120 TPS Flash, 50 TPS Pro**. Default Markov r = 256; parallel drafters noted as able to use blocks as large as γ = 16 without proportionally increasing latency.
- **Timing:** **Model before paper** — the checkpoints (HF repos created **2026-06-27**) were published ~9 days before this paper, and the paper reports online deployment "within the DeepSeek-V4 serving system under live user traffic" against MTP-1.

### DreamCraft3D: Hierarchical 3D Generation with Bootstrapped Diffusion Prior
[arXiv:2310.16818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16818) · v1 2023-10-25; v2 2023-10-26 · ✅ verified-page · *multimodal*

- **Why:** 2D-lifted 3D generation suffered the "Janus issue" (individually plausible views that are inconsistent in the round) and texture over-smoothing from the large CFG weights SDS needs. This splits the problem into geometry sculpting and texture boosting so a **scene-specific** prior can enforce view consistency.
- **Mechanisms:** Generate a 2D reference from a text prompt, then lift in two cascaded stages. **Geometry sculpting** with a view-conditioned 3D prior (off-the-shelf Zero-1-to-3) giving `L_3D-SDS`, combined with a 2D prior (DeepFloyd IF base at 64×64) into `L_hybrid = L_SDS + μ·L_3D-SDS` with **μ = 2**; photometric, mask, depth (negative Pearson correlation), and normal losses anchor the reference view. **Progressive view training** enlarges the training views to propagate geometry to 360°. **Diffusion timestep annealing**: sample t from [0.7, 0.85] early to fix global structure, anneal linearly to [0.2, 0.5] over hundreds of iterations. Coarse-to-fine: optimize an implicit surface (NeuS) first, then initialize a textured mesh via **DMTet**. **Bootstrapped Score Distillation (`L_BSD`)**: fine-tune a personalized diffusion model (DreamBooth) on noise-augmented multi-view renderings of the scene, with each view's camera parameter as an extra condition, **alternating** optimization of prior and scene (twice in experiments). The texture stage uses variational score distillation with Stable Diffusion and **drops** the Zero-1-to-3 prior because it hurts texture quality.
- **Numbers:** Instant NGP optimized from 64 to 384 resolution; DMTet at a 128 grid with 512 rendering resolution; DeepFloyd IF prior at 64×64. μ = 2; annealing [0.7, 0.85] → [0.2, 0.5]; BSD alternation twice. Table 1 metrics (CLIP up, "Contextual" down) measured on **300 generated samples** — **per-method values were truncated and are NOT reported.** Baselines: DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer, Make-it-3D, Magic123.
- **Timing:** **UNKNOWN** — a 3D-generation method making no claim about landing in a DeepSeek LLM.
- **Boundary note:** **DeepSeek-adjacent, not a DeepSeek model paper** — only **2 of 7 authors** (Wen Liu, Zhenda Xie) are DeepSeek-affiliated; the rest are Tsinghua or an independent researcher. ### Janus: Decoupling Visual Encoding for Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation
[arXiv:2410.13848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13848) · 2024-10-17 (v1 only) · ✅ verified-page · *multimodal*

- **Why:** Unified understanding-plus-generation models typically force one visual encoder to serve both tasks, and understanding suffers because the two need different information granularity. Janus removes the conflict by decoupling the visual paths while keeping a **single transformer**.
- **Mechanisms:** **Decoupled visual encoding** — a separate understanding pathway (SigLIP-Large-Patch16-384 features flattened 2-D→1-D through an understanding adaptor) and a separate generation pathway (VQ tokenizer with a 16,384-entry codebook downsampling 16×, codebook embeddings mapped by a generation adaptor), both feeding one unified autoregressive transformer. Single transformer over the concatenated multimodal feature sequence, using the LLM's built-in head for text and a **randomly initialized head** for images, with **no specially designed attention masks**. **Three-stage training**: Stage I trains only the two adaptors and the image head (encoders and LLM frozen); Stage II unfreezes the LLM and mixes pure text, multimodal understanding, and visual generation, starting with ImageNet-1k generation before open-domain text-to-image; Stage III trains all parameters except the generation encoder, masking system and user prompts. **Classifier-free guidance** at inference with default scale 5, enabled by replacing the text condition with a pad token at 10% probability during training. Both adaptors are two-layer MLPs; sequence packing is used.
- **Numbers:** Understanding (1.3B, built on DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B): MMBench **69.4**, SEED-Bench **63.7**, POPE **87.0** — stated to outperform LLaVA-v1.5 (7B) and Qwen-VL-Chat (7B). Generation: MSCOCO-30K **FID 8.53**, GenEval **61%** — stated to surpass DALL-E 2 and SDXL. Training cost: **7 days on 16 nodes × 8 A100-40GB**, HAI-LLM. Codebook 16,384, 16× downsampling; images 384×384; LLM max sequence length 4,096. Schedule (Table 1): steps 10,000 / 180,000 / 24,000; batch 256 / 512 / 256; LR 1.0e-3 / 1e-4 / 2.0e-5; warm-up 300 / 5,000 / 0; data ratio (understanding:text:generation) 1:0:1 / 2:3:5 / 7:3:10.

### JanusFlow: Harmonizing Autoregression and Rectified Flow for Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation
[arXiv:2411.07975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07975) · v1 2024-11-12; v2 2025-03-24 · ✅ verified-page · *multimodal*

- **Why:** Prior unified models either bolted an external diffusion model onto an LLM (complex, generation capped by the external model) or quantized images into discrete tokens (capped by tokenizer quality). This shows a **rectified-flow generative head can be trained directly inside a standard LLM** with only lightweight adapters and no special attention masking.
- **Mechanisms:** **Rectified flow inside the LLM framework** — the LLM itself predicts the velocity field; the stated key finding is that "rectified flow can be straightforwardly trained within the large language model framework, eliminating the need for complex architectural modifications." ### DeepSeek-OCR 2: Visual Causal Flow
[arXiv:2601.20552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20552) · 2026-01-28 (v1 only) · ✅ verified-page · *multimodal*

- **Why:** Conventional VLMs feed visual tokens to the LLM in rigid raster-scan order with fixed positional encoding, contradicting human vision's semantically driven scanning of complex layouts. This asks whether 2D image understanding can instead be achieved by **two cascaded 1D causal reasoning structures** — an encoder that reorders visual tokens by image semantics before the LLM reads them.
- **Mechanisms:** **DeepEncoder V2**: the CLIP component of DeepEncoder is replaced by a compact LLM-style architecture (instantiated from Qwen2-0.5B-base), giving **dual-stream attention** — visual tokens use bidirectional attention to preserve CLIP-style global modelling while new **causal flow queries** use causal attention. **Causal flow tokens**: learnable queries appended after the visual tokens as a suffix, **one per visual token** (`W*H/(16²·16)`), each attending to all visual tokens and to preceding queries; only the causal query outputs (the latter half of encoder outputs) are projected to the LLM decoder. **Custom attention mask** `M = [[1_{m×m}, 0_{m×n}], [1_{n×m}, LowerTri(n)]]` with n = m — a bidirectional ViT-like block over visual tokens concatenated along the sequence dimension with a causal lower-triangular block over flow tokens. The paper reports a **cross-attention mBART-style variant "fail to converge"**, attributed to insufficient visual-token interaction, whereas prefix-concatenation keeps visual tokens active through all layers. **Multi-crop token budget**: global 1024×1024 → **256** query embeddings; local 768×768 with k = 0..6 crops **sharing a unified set of 144** query embeddings, so tokens to the LLM are `k·144 + 256` ∈ **[256, 1120]** — below DeepSeek-OCR's 1156 (Gundam) and matching Gemini-3-Pro's maximum. **Carried over**: 80M SAM-base plus two convolutional layers with output dim reduced 1024 → **896**, giving 16× compression via window attention; the decoder is **deliberately not upgraded** (DeepSeek-OCR's 3B MoE, ~500M active). Three stages: encoder pretraining (language-modelling objective with a lightweight decoder), query enhancement (freeze tokenizer, jointly optimize LLM encoder + decoder), decoder specialization (freeze all DeepEncoder V2 parameters, update only the DeepSeek-LLM).
- **Numbers:** **3.73% gain on OmniDocBench v1.5** vs the DeepSeek-OCR baseline. Visual token budget **256–1120**; 1120 vs DeepSeek-OCR's 1156 and matching Gemini-3-Pro's maximum. **16× token compression** from an 80M-parameter tokenizer, "comparable to the typical 100M parameters used for text input embeddings in LLMs." Encoder: Qwen2-0.5B gives **500M**, "comparable to CLIP ViT (300M)" which it replaces. Stage 1: AdamW, cosine decay 1e-4 → 1e-6, **160 A100 GPUs**, batch 640, 40k iterations with packing at 8K, ~100M image-text pairs; dataloaders at 768×768 and 1024×1024. Stage 2: 160 GPUs at 40GB, 40 data-parallel replicas, global batch 1280, 15k iterations, cosine decay 5e-5 → 1e-6, 4-stage pipeline parallelism. Stage 3: 20k iterations, decay 1e-6 → 5e-8, and "more than doubles the training speed under the same global batch size" because the encoder is frozen. OCR is **80%** of the mixture; OCR 1.0 pages partitioned text/formula/table at **3:1:1**.
- **Timing:** Explicitly positions itself **after** the first DeepSeek-OCR model: "Following DeepSeek-OCR [53], we adopt document reading as our primary experimental testbed"; the decoder is retained unchanged and "Given these minimal differences, we consider DeepSeek-OCR a valid baseline for comparison." The first OCR paper's ID and date were **not fetched in this task**, so the gap is **UNKNOWN** and not asserted here (see the entry above for 2510.18234, 2025-10-21 — a cross-entry link, not a claim from this paper).
- **Caveats:** Exactly **3 authors**: Haoran Wei, Yaofeng Sun, Yukun Li. Affiliation line gives **DeepSeek-AI only**. Submitter Haoran Wei; CC BY 4.0. **Fetch truncated inside Table 1, so DeepSeek-OCR 2's own OmniDocBench OVERALL score was NOT read — only the +3.73% delta is asserted.** Per-model scores visible before truncation (PaddleOCR-VL 92.86, MinerU2.5 90.67, MonkeyOCR-pro-3B 88.85, PP-StructureV3 86.73, Dolphin-1.5 83.21, Marker-1.8.2 71.30) are **baseline/competitor rows, not DeepSeek-OCR 2's score.** §6 discusses "Towards Genuine 2D Reasoning" and "Towards Native Multimodality" as **future work**, not delivered capabilities.

### Thinking with Visual Primitives
Technical report (no arXiv) · 2026-04-30 · ✅ verified-page (via Zenodo + HF mirror) · *multimodal*

- **Why:** Names the **"Reference Gap"** — NL chain-of-thought cannot unambiguously point at objects in dense spatial layouts, causing logical collapse — and proposes grounding the reasoning trajectory itself in image coordinates instead of only improving perception.

## Evolution narrative: from matching the frontier to owning the post-training stack

**Phase 1 (late 2023 – mid 2024): match the frontier with open weights, and publish the recipe.** DeepSeek enters as a lab that releases weights first and papers second: the `deepseek-coder-6.7b-base` card lands 2023-10-23, the DeepSeek LLM cards 2023-11-29, and the matching papers arrive 2024-01-25 and 2024-01-05. The papers are unusually candid about method. DeepSeek LLM re-derives scaling laws *including* hyperparameter scaling (`B_opt = 0.2920·C^0.3271`, `eta_opt = 0.3118·C^-0.1250`), replaces `C = 6ND` with `C = MD` using a non-embedding FLOPs/token measure, and swaps the cosine schedule for a multi-step one specifically so the first phase can be reused for continual training — an engineering decision disguised as a hyperparameter choice. DeepSeek-Coder documents repository-level data construction and FIM. Neither claims a new paradigm; both claim competitive open models.

**Phase 2 (January – December 2024): win on architecture and efficiency.** Two component papers become the load-bearing walls of everything after. DeepSeekMoE (2024-01-11) argues expert granularity and always-on shared experts matter more than expert count, with a clean combinatorial argument — C(16,2)=120 versus C(64,8)=4,426,165,368 — validated at 2B, 16B, and a preliminary 145B. The auxiliary-loss-free balancer (2024-08-28) removes the interference gradients load balancing normally costs, updating a per-expert bias by `sign` of the load violation. Then DeepSeek-V2 (2024-05-07) fuses both with **MLA**, cutting KV cache by 93.3% and training cost by 42.5% versus DeepSeek 67B. V2 is the pivot: from here, efficiency is the lab's identity. Coder-V2 (2024-06-17) immediately reuses the whole V2 stack — intermediate checkpoint, tokenizer, GRPO alignment, Yarn hyper-parameters — scaled to 236B/21B with 338 languages and 128K context. That reuse is the template: V2's components become defaults, not experiments.

The architectural line then forks. One branch is **sparsity as a first-class training objective**: NSA (2025-02-16) makes sparse attention natively trainable with group-consistent GQA selection so memory-access sparsity matches compute sparsity. The other branch is the one that shipped: **DSA** debuts inside the V3.2-Exp *model* on 2025-09-29, with only a model card and a GitHub PDF, reaching arXiv two months later. By December 2025 V3.2 scales GRPO, adds a 1,800-environment agentic data pipeline, and reports GPT-5-class reasoning. By April 2026 the V4 report assembles CSA + HCA + mHC + Muon at 1M context, and by September 2026 V4.1-Flash pushes KV to 890 bytes/token with CSA2 cross-layer reuse and FP4 caching — a 437-fold reduction against V1. Notably, each generation's *headline* is a cache or FLOPs number, not a benchmark.

**Phase 3 (February 2024 – January 2025): win on RL for reasoning.** DeepSeekMath is the hinge of the company's later reputation, and its contribution is one algorithmic idea: **GRPO** drops PPO's critic and estimates the baseline from group scores. The paper's own framing is modest — a memory optimization for a 7B math model — but the consequences are not. V2 adopts it for Chat (RL) in May 2024. Prover-V1.5 uses it to turn Lean's binary verification into a reward signal (RLPAF) and adds RMaxTS to explore proof paths, reaching 63.5% on miniF2F-test with tree search. Then R1 (January 2025) makes the decisive demonstration: **pure RL with rule-based rewards, no human reasoning trajectories, and for R1-Zero no SFT at all.** The paper is explicit that neural outcome and process reward models were rejected because they "may suffer from reward hacking." Everything downstream — the 800k-sample rejection-sampling SFT set, the language-consistency reward, the six distilled dense models — is engineering around that one bet. R1 becomes a Nature paper in September 2025 (with a **different title**, worth noting for citation hygiene), and V3's own v2 text then distills R1's reasoning *back* into V3.

The same logic is then pushed into domains where correctness is checkable. Prover (May 2024) and Prover-V2 (April 2025) automate formalization and use subgoal decomposition plus GRPO with a consistency reward to reach 88.9% on miniF2F-test. Inference-Time Scaling for Generalist Reward Modeling (April 2025) attacks the harder case — non-verifiable domains — with SPCT and the DeepSeek-GRM family, showing inference-time voting over a generative reward model (+4.9 over greedy at 32 samples) can substitute for training-time scale. DeepSeekMath-V2 (November 2025) closes the loop by making the reward model *self-critical*: a verifier is trained against rubrics, a meta-verifier suppresses its hallucinated criticisms, and the generator is rewarded for finding and fixing its own issues before finalizing (α = 0.76, β = 0.24). The result is 118/120 on Putnam 2024 and gold-level IMO 2025 / CMO 2024 **without final-answer supervision**, which is the point.

**Phase 4 (August 2024 – September 2026): win on systems, then on environments.** Systems work is present early but becomes strategic later. Fire-Flyer AI-HPC (2024-08-26) documents 10,000 PCIe A100s at ~83% of DGX-A100 GEMM performance for 60% of the cost, with 122 switches where the DGX-equivalent needs 1,320 — a statement about capital efficiency that prefigures the V3 training-cost claims. The V3 report advertises $5.576M for 2.788M H800 GPU hours, coupled with FP8 mixed precision and DualPipe overlap. The hardware-reflection paper (ISCA '25, 2025-05-14) then quantifies what is actually binding: MLA's 70.272 KB/token KV cache against 327.680 KB for Qwen-2.5 72B and 516.096 KB for LLaMA-3.1 405B, 250 GFLOPS/token versus 2,448 for a 405B dense model, and an all-to-all step cost of 120.96 µs on CX7 that would fall to 6.72 µs on GB200 NVL72 — a theoretical 67 → 1,200 tokens/s gap that is really an argument about interconnects.

The final phase extends the same engineering discipline to *environments*. V3.2's report already frames agentic capability as a **data-synthesis** problem ("over 1,800 distinct environments and 85,000 complex prompts") rather than a modeling problem. DSec (September 2026) makes that concrete at infrastructure scale: ~160 nodes per unit, 3 million sandboxes per day, 380,000 concurrent, 5,000 creations per second, with sandboxes **suspended across preemptive RL training** so rollout state survives and idle capacity is reclaimed — a 1.7× completion-time penalty for eager image pulling versus 57% fewer disk writes for on-demand loading. DSpark (July 2026) is the serving-side counterpart: already in production before its paper, reporting +60–85% per-user generation speed at matched aggregate throughput via semi-autoregressive drafting and confidence-scheduled verification. These last two papers are not about making the model smarter — they are about making long-horizon agentic work *economic*.

**Connective tissue.** Three things recur. **Reuse over reinvention**: MLA and DeepSeekMoE from V2 appear in V3, V3.1, V3.2, V4, VL2, and OCR; GRPO runs from February 2024 to December 2025. **Publish the component before the flagship**: the auxiliary-loss-free balancer, mHC, and Engram each appear as standalone papers months before a model card names them. **Ship before you can cite**: V3.1, V3.1-Terminus, V3.2-Exp, R1-0528, DSpark, and the entire V2.5/V3-0324 line reached users with no citable paper, and in two cases the technique was in production before the write-up existed. A reader who tracks only arXiv will systematically lag the models by one to three months, and in the V3.1 case will never see them at all.

### The GRPO lineage in one line

`DeepSeekMath 2024-02-05 (introduces GRPO, 7B math)` → `V2 Chat 2024-05-07 (first product adoption)` → `Coder-V2 2024-06-17 (GRPO + reward model instead of compiler 0/1)` → `Prover-V1.5 2024-08-15 (GRPO + Lean 0/1 as RLPAF)` → `R1 2025-01-22 (GRPO + rule-based rewards, `(r−mean)/std`)` → `V3-0324 2025-03 (Keep Routing added)` → `V3.2 2025-12-02 (GRPO patched: no std division, unbiased KL, off-policy masking, keep sampling mask)`. **The algorithm was never replaced — 22 months of patching.**

## Third-party work that extends or contests these techniques

**These are NOT DeepSeek papers.** They are listed because they are the clearest evidence of which DeepSeek techniques the field considered load-bearing enough to build on — and, in one case, to correct. Every entry was fetched and verified; authors are external in each case.

| Third-party paper | Target | What it does | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| **mHC-lite: You Don't Need 20 Sinkhorn-Knopp Iterations** — [2601.05732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05732) | mHC ([2512.24880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880)) | **Contests the mechanism.** Identifies two limitations of DeepSeek's approach: finite Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations do not guarantee exact double stochasticity (an approximation gap that can accumulate with depth), and efficient SK needs specialised CUDA kernels, hurting portability. Reconstructs doubly stochastic matrices explicitly as convex combinations of permutation matrices via Birkhoff–von Neumann, needing only native matrix ops. Reports matching or exceeding mHC with higher throughput. 2 authors; submitted **2026-01-09**, nine days after mHC. | ✅ abs page |
| **Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective** (Dr. GRPO) — [2503.20783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783) | R1 / GRPO ([2402.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)) | **Contests the mechanism.** Shows GRPO's std and per-sequence length normalizations create an optimization bias that inflates response length, especially for *incorrect* outputs, and removes both. | **DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale** — [2503.14476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476) | GRPO | Four patches to GRPO: asymmetric clip-higher, dynamic sampling (discard zero-variance groups), token-level loss normalization, overlong filtering/shaping. ## Verifying this list yourself: three queries, and one trap

This document's completeness was checked against the **arXiv export API**, not against search engines. The queries below are reproducible; each returns Atom XML. **Do not trust the `totalResults` field alone — read the entries.**

**Query 1 — the collective author field.**
```
https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=au:"DeepSeek-AI"
  AND+submittedDate:[202604010000+TO+202609302359]
```
Returns `totalResults = 2`: DeepSeek-V4 ([2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348)) and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ([2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)). Both are in this document.

**Query 2 — a named author who appears on the flagship reports.**
```
https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=au:"Wenfeng+Liang"
  AND+submittedDate:[202601010000+TO+202609302359]
```
Returns `totalResults = 5`, and **all five are in this document**: Engram ([2601.07372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372), 2026-01-12), DeepSeek-V4 ([2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348), 2026-04-26), DSpark ([2607.05147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05147), 2026-07-06), DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ([2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969), 2026-09-17), DSec ([2609.22978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22978), 2026-09-19).

### ⚠️ The trap: neither query alone is complete

**These two queries disagree, and the disagreement is the finding.**

- Query 1 misses 3 of the 5 papers — Engram, DSpark, and DSec **do not carry the collective "DeepSeek-AI" author entry at all**; they list individual authors only. DSec (131 authors) and DSpark (33 authors) are DeepSeek papers by any reading, yet `au:"DeepSeek-AI"` returns neither.
- Query 2 cannot stand alone either, because Wenfeng Liang is **absent** from DeepSeekMath, Prover, Prover-V1.5, Prover-V2, DeepSeekMath-V2, DeepSeek-OCR, DeepSeek-OCR 2, DeepSeek-VL, ESFT, CodeI/O, GRM, Janus/JanusFlow/Janus-Pro, VL2, and DreamCraft3D. A Liang-only enumeration would miss the entire math and multimodal branches.

**Consequence:** any list built from a single author-field query — in either direction — is provably incomplete. The two queries must be **unioned**, and even then the union is not guaranteed complete, because a DeepSeek paper with neither the collective author entry nor Liang would evade both. Two further angles are therefore also recorded in Sources: a `ti:"DeepSeek"` title query (130 hits) and the official `api-docs.deepseek.com` release-note index, which is the only way to catch releases that have no paper at all.

**A fourth, weaker angle, and why it is not usable on its own.** `all:"DeepSeek"` over 2026 returns `totalResults = 1101` — almost entirely third-party papers that merely *evaluate* or *cite* DeepSeek models (e.g. jailbreak benchmarks, financial benchmarks, RTL test-plan generation). It is useful only for finding third-party follow-up work such as the table above; it cannot enumerate DeepSeek's own output, and its count should not be compared with the two author queries.

### What "complete" can and cannot mean here

Three residual risks are stated rather than papered over:

1. **Q1 2026 returned `totalResults = 0`** for `au:"DeepSeek-AI"`, while Engram ([2601.07372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372), 2026-01-12) is a real DeepSeek paper from that window. The collective author field is applied inconsistently by submitters, so **a zero result from it is not evidence of absence.**
2. **Preprints with no arXiv presence at all** are invisible to every query here. The V3.2-Exp GitHub PDF and the V4 HF-hosted PDF are both instances already noted in this document.
3. **Completeness is asserted only against arXiv**, whose author metadata is demonstrably unreliable for this org. Coverage of the non-arXiv surface rests on the release-note and model-card sweeps in Section VI, which are bounded by what those pages list.

## Reading order: eight papers for the technical evolution

1. **DeepSeek LLM** — [2401.02954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02954). Read for the scaling-law method and the multi-step LR scheduler: it establishes that this lab publishes its *reasoning about hyperparameters*, not just its results.
2. **DeepSeekMoE** — [2401.06066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066). The expert-granularity and shared-expert vocabulary every later model uses; the combinatorial argument is the cleanest statement of why fine-grained MoE wins.
3. **DeepSeek-V2** — [2405.04434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434). **MLA** plus the V2 DeepSeekMoE configuration, plus the 93.3% KV-cache number that makes every subsequent efficiency claim legible.
4. **DeepSeekMath** — [2402.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300). **GRPO's origin**, in a modest 7B math paper. Everything in phases 3 and 4 of the narrative descends from this.
5. **DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report** — [2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437). The integration point: MLA + DeepSeekMoE + auxiliary-loss-free balancing + FP8 + DualPipe + MTP, with a cost table. Read §3.2.1 for DualPipe and §3.2.2 for the all-to-all kernels DeepEP later open-sourced.
6. **DeepSeek-R1** — [2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). The pure-RL result and the deliberate rejection of neural reward models; the multi-stage pipeline is the template for every later post-training recipe.
7. **DeepSeek-V3.2** — [2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556). The convergence: DSA for long context, "Scaling GRPO" for stability, agentic task synthesis as a data problem. Where architecture, RL, and environments become one story.
8. **DeepSeek-V4** — [2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348). The current architecture of record (CSA + HCA + mHC + Muon) and the claim that defines the generation: 27% of V3.2's single-token FLOPs and 10% of its KV cache at 1M context.

**Two additions depending on interest.** For theorem proving, insert **Prover-V2** ([2504.21801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21801)) after R1, then **DeepSeekMath-V2** ([2511.22570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22570)) last; together they show how verification became a reward model and then became self-critical. For the agentic-infrastructure turn, read **DSpark** ([2607.05147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05147)) and **DSec** ([2609.22978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22978)) as a pair — serving throughput and sandboxed environments are the two constraints bounding long-horizon agent work.

**Read alongside, not instead:** the DeepSeek-V2 and V3.2-Exp **model cards**, because DSA's release-side description and the MLA/V2 architecture details reached users through cards before (or without) papers.
